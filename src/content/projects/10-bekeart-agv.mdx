## Chassis and mass budget

Everything downstream is sized against the mass it has to carry, so the budget came first.

| Parameter | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Expected total AGV weight | 90 kg |
| Additional design allowance | 7–8 kg |
| Preliminary design mass | 97–98 kg |
| Chassis weight | 25 kg |
| Wheel diameter | 152 mm |

The 152 mm wheel matters more than it looks: it sets both the linear speed per motor RPM and the
encoder resolution needed to hold a straight line. It also fixes the turning geometry, so the
chassis is drawn around the wheelbase rather than the other way round.

## Drive chain

Four motors, each on its own driver, so a fault or a trim on one side can never be masked by
the other three.

| Parameter | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Motor | Orange PG56/63ZY125-2440-65K |
| Supply | 24 V DC |
| Rated speed | 49 RPM |
| Rated torque | 21 Nm per motor |
| Rated current | 9 A per motor |
| Total rated current | 36 A for four motors |

Drivers are 4 × SmartElex 30S — 30 A continuous, 85 A peak, 28 V maximum operating voltage.

> **Open constraint.** The pack reaches 29.2 V fully charged, which is above the driver's
> 28 V ceiling, so the SmartElex 30S cannot sit directly across the battery bank. The choice
> is either a driver rated for the higher voltage or an engineered regulated motor supply;
> either way it has to be closed before the pack is built.

## Power: 2S4P LiFePO4

Eight Pro-Range LiFePO4 cells at 12.8 V / 30 Ah, wired 2S4P — two in series per string, four
strings in parallel.

| Parameter | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Nominal voltage | 25.6 V |
| Capacity | 120 Ah |
| Total energy | 3,072 Wh |
| Fully charged voltage | 29.2 V |
| Estimated battery weight | 25.6 kg |

Two things have to be confirmed with the manufacturer before this is built as drawn: that the
individual BMS units tolerate a 2S4P configuration, and that the cells are matched well enough
to share current across parallel strings. A purpose-built 25.6 V / 120 Ah pack is the fallback
if the individual units will not.

### Runtime

Target was 6–8 hours. At 80 % usable energy:

| Average power consumption | Backup |
| --- | --- |
| 300 W | 8.2 hours |
| 350 W | 7 hours |
| 400 W | 6.1 hours |
| 500 W | 4.9 hours |

Real runtime depends on motor load, how much the rover moves, stops and turns, and how hard
the Jetson is working — the table is the floor, not a promise.

## Compute

The NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano 8 GB handles AI and camera processing, fed from a DC-DC buck
converter that takes the main pack down to a regulated 12 V at 5 A (as tested). No separate
battery is needed, which keeps the mass budget intact.

## Encoder feedback

All four motors take incremental A/B magnetic encoders. The STM32 uses them to measure real
motor speed, synchronise all four motors, and correct the difference between wheel speeds.
That last part is the whole point: with raw PWM the left and right wheels drift apart within
seconds, and the rover curves off the line. With the loop closed, the commanded speed is a
speed and not a duty cycle.

## Telemetry dashboard

The operator surface for the platform — chassis monitor on top, then the working tabs.
Screens below are the real dashboard running against the platform.

![Chassis monitor — per-motor driver, PWM, direction and encoder counts](/images/projects/screens/bekeart-agv.webp)

The chassis monitor is the always-on view: target against measured wheel speed, applied PWM,
and live encoder counts for all four motors.

![Drive tab — annotated camera feed with line tracking](/images/projects/screens/bekeart-agv-2.webp)

The drive view puts the annotated camera feed under the monitor, with the line offset, angle,
lookahead and curvature the decision loop is actually working from.

![Motor test — each driver on its own](/images/projects/screens/bekeart-agv-4.webp)

The motor test tab drives one side at a time, in encoder-PID or raw-PWM mode, with a 0 → 100 → 0
ramp. It is the bench check that a driver, a motor and an encoder agree before the robot is
allowed near a line.

![LiDAR obstacle safety — stop, slow and clear zones](/images/projects/screens/bekeart-agv-5.webp)

LiDAR safety runs three zones: stop, slow and clear. A sensor that goes stale or drops off
stops the rover rather than letting it drive blind.

![Recording — frames plus a detection log](/images/projects/screens/bekeart-agv-6.webp)

Recording captures the camera frames and a log row per frame, so a fault can be traced back to
the frame that caused it.
