## Ingestion is half the problem

Retail data arrives in whatever shape the vendor had. The dataset registry resolves it:
a column-alias map folds `sales`, `units_sold`, `qty` and `demand` onto one field, the
frame is re-aggregated to `(store_id, product_id, date)`, and fields the file did not
carry are derived — `competitor_price` from `price`, `seasonality` from the month,
`inventory_level` as seven days of sales when it is missing. CSV, JSON and XLSX all go
in.

The underlying dataset is **73,100 rows** across five stores and twenty products over two
years, of which 14,611 rows form the grocery subset and 100 store-product series.

## Zero-demand is censored data, not zero

The subtlest thing in the forecasting code is what it does with a row where inventory
was at or below zero *and* nothing was sold. That is not a demand of zero — it is a
demand that was never observable, because the shelf was empty. Those rows are set to
missing and interpolated with `limit_direction="both"`, rather than being trained on as
zeros.

## Uncertainty, and when it goes missing

Prediction intervals come from split-conformal regression where MAPIE is available, and
fall back to a Gaussian interval built from the validation residual spread otherwise.
The committed model reports a validation residual standard deviation of 65.8 alongside
its MAE and RMSE.

Drift is watched separately. A hand-rolled **Population Stability Index** over ten
bins on six numeric features compares a 60-day baseline against a recent 14-day window,
with severity thresholds at PSI 0.1 / 0.25 / 0.5 and mean-shift thresholds at 15 / 30 /
50%. A scan history keeps the last hundred readings.

## Causal, and honest about its own uncertainty

Most forecasters answer "what will demand be." Foretell Vista also answers "what moved
it" — and is careful about the answer. Three independent estimators are run and
combined by inverse-variance proxy weighting:

| Estimator | Approach |
| --- | --- |
| PSM | Propensity score from a logistic model, matched by nearest neighbour |
| DML | Random-forest nuisance estimates, residual regression on a logistic model |
| DiD | Treated and control `store × product` groups differenced over time |

When those three disagree, the platform says so. On the shipped data the spread between
them is **187%** — and the response carries a warning rather than a single confident
number. Feature attribution runs through SHAP where it is available, with a
permutation-importance fallback, and the causal factor breakdown reports price, month,
season and weather contributions directly.

## The product graph

Complements and substitutes are the same correlation with opposite signs, so the graph
is signed: a positive correlation is an edge from a complement, a negative one a
substitute. Nodes are the top SKUs by rank, tiered into anchor, core, growth and niche
by rank ratio, with a fixed spring-layout seed so the picture does not move between
renders. There is a fallback that keeps the strongest edges when the correlation
threshold would otherwise produce an empty graph.

Embeddings come from an SVD of the signed adjacency matrix — spectral vectors scaled
by singular value, then combined with the feature matrix. It is not message passing,
and it is not a learned GNN; it is a cheap way to get the graph structure into the same
feature space as the demand features.

## Replenishment, measured against a baseline

Inventory is simulated in SimPy, and a PPO policy trained with Stable-Baselines3 is
compared against a fixed heuristic on the same episodes. Over eight series the RL
policy reaches a **12.8% lower total inventory cost** than the (s,S) baseline, with
average inventory down from 1,842 to 1,607 units. The service level is held at 100%
for both, so the saving is not bought with stockouts.

The trained policy is evaluated against a served heuristic in production, and the
comparison is what the platform reports — the number on the dashboard is the difference
between two policies on the same world model, not a score in isolation.

## Scope

The federated layer simulates rounds of cross-silo aggregation over the store
partitions — five rounds, four participants per round — and the project's own task list
records that it should be replaced with true cross-client training. That is stated here
rather than dressed up: the current implementation approximates the privacy-preserving
workflow, and the replacement is the next piece of work.
